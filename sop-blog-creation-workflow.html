Standard Operating Procedure

Blog Creation Workflow

Person Responsible: David Version: 1.0 Date: May 2026

Overview

This SOP covers how a Timar blog post goes from idea to live on the website. The process is designed so David records a voice memo, Claude writes the full article and blog HTML, and David does one upload to GitHub. Netlify publishes it automatically in 30 seconds. No developer needed.

Types of Blog Posts

All blogs follow the Content Gain principle - they must contain information not found elsewhere. There are two main types:

Suburb Profiles

Is Sandy Bay a good suburb? Kingston property market. West Hobart investment guide. Targets people researching where to buy - often shows up in ChatGPT answers.

Market Commentary

Negative gearing changes explained. Winter market update. Budget impact on investors. Targets people in decision mode - converts well to leads.

Both types use the same process. The only difference is the trigger - suburb blogs start with a Whisperflow memo, market commentary blogs can start from a recording or a conversation (like the Fathom call transcript).

Step-by-Step Process

1

Record Your Memo (David)

Open Whisperflow and record 60-90 seconds of intel on the topic. For suburb blogs, follow the Suburb Content Memos SOP. For market commentary, speak freely about the topic - your interpretation, your take, what you're seeing on the ground.

Even a rough 60-second recording is enough. Don't script it - authenticity is the point. Claude handles the structure and writing.
2

Send the Transcript to Claude (David)

Copy the Whisperflow transcript and paste it into a Claude conversation. Use this prompt:

Write a blog post for Timar Buyers Agency about [Sandy Bay / negative gearing / topic] using this memo: [paste transcript]

Claude will:

  • Write a full 600-900 word article in David's voice
  • Format it as a complete HTML file matching the Timar website style
  • Create an SEO-optimised title and meta description
  • Include a clear call to action at the end
  • Also update blog.html to add the new post card to the blog index
3

Review the Draft (David)

Read through the blog quickly. Check:

  • The intel from your memo is accurately represented
  • The tone sounds like you (direct, professional, not salesy)
  • No figures or facts have been invented - only what you provided

If anything needs changing, tell Claude and it will fix it. Once happy, download both HTML files Claude provides.

Claude will give you two files: the new blog post (e.g. blog-sandy-bay-suburb-profile.html) and the updated blog.html. You need both.
4

Upload to GitHub (David)

Go to github.com and open the timar-website repository.

To upload the new blog file:

  • Click "Add file" then "Upload files"
  • Drag in the new blog HTML file (e.g. blog-sandy-bay-suburb-profile.html)
  • Click "Commit changes"

To update blog.html:

  • Click on blog.html in the repository
  • Click the pencil (Edit) icon
  • Select all, delete, paste in the updated blog.html content Claude gave you
  • Click "Commit changes"
5

Netlify Publishes Automatically

Netlify detects the GitHub change and redeploys within 30 seconds. The new blog post is live at timar.com.au/blog-sandy-bay-suburb-profile and the blog index page is updated.

No further action needed. The page is indexed by Google automatically once live.

Content Standards

Every blog must meet these standards before publishing:

  • No invented figures - only use data David has provided or that is publicly available
  • New information - at least one piece of intel not easily found on other websites (this is what drives Content Gain ranking)
  • David's voice - direct, professional, no hype or cliches
  • Clear CTA - every post ends with an invitation to book a call or get in touch
  • No em dashes - use hyphens or colons instead
  • Both agents - where relevant, reference the Timar team (David and Ben), not just David

Blog Naming Convention

File names must be consistent for clean URLs on the website:

  • Suburb profiles: blog-[suburb]-[state]-suburb-profile.html (e.g. blog-sandy-bay-tasmania-suburb-profile.html)
  • Market commentary: blog-[topic-slug].html (e.g. blog-negative-gearing-changes-2026.html)
  • All lowercase, hyphens between words, no spaces or special characters

Target Cadence

Aim for 2 new blog posts per week. At this pace, Timar builds a library of 100+ suburb and market articles within 12 months - each one a permanent lead-generating asset on the website and in AI search results.
